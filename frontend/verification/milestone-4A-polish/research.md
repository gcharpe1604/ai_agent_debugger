## Design decision after research

**P:** use a shared, unframed 384 px account form, optically centered between a quiet product header and a small development notice. Give headings more weight than helper copy, align all controls, integrate the visibility button, and keep the same vertical rhythm for login/registration. Use the same composition for expiration/denial with one dominant recovery action. The notice explicitly says authentication is disconnected; sample routes remain available.

**P:** project pages stay inside the debugger shell. A compact bordered form separates section heading/fields from its action footer; read-only Project ID is distinct from the name. Empty workspaces explain one next step. Fixture disclosure moves below primary content and still states that changes reset and confer no ownership/access. Do not introduce a secondary settings sidebar with one section, fake configuration controls, security claims or investigator changes.

These are synthesized product decisions, not competitor capabilities or copied branding. Competitor failure handling, mobile accessibility, credential submission and successful onboarding were not tested. Current live references are dark; our coordinated light version follows existing semantic tokens, not a claimed competitor light implementation.

## Signup usability follow-up — 17 October 2026

**D (opened official guidance):** [W3C multi-page forms](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/multi-page/) recommends logical groups, explicit progress and a step-aware page title. [GOV.UK validation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/) recommends actionable input errors and distinguishes validation from access/eligibility decisions. These are usability references, not evidence of a competitor's behavior or a claim of accessibility conformance.

**P:** retain the user-approved two steps; replace the quiet progress sentence with a labeled ordered indicator, mark the current step, update the document title and show the chosen email on step two. Keep the column top stable across steps, remove unnecessary bottom padding, preserve required-field/password-confirmation errors and first-invalid focus. No third review step, invented username/password policy, provider login or new backend behavior. Existing competitor observations above remain the visual basis; competitors were not re-audited for this follow-up.
